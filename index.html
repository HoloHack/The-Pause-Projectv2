01 / RECOGNISEUNDERSTAND THE PATTERN, NOT JUST THE POST.

Look beyond the screen.

Bullying is more than an unpleasant exchange. In Australian schools, it means an ongoing, deliberate misuse of power through repeated behaviour that can cause harm. Online, a single post can be shared again and again — making the impact continue.

Australian national definition Bullying No Way ↗

I / POWER

Power matters.

A real or perceived advantage — such as status, influence or group numbers — can make it difficult to defend yourself.

II / REPETITION
↻↻

Patterns matter.

Bullying is repeated or has potential to recur. One online post may be repeatedly circulated or revisited.

III / HARM
!

Impact matters.

Verbal, physical, social and online behaviours can damage someone's safety, friendships, confidence and participation.

↳

Important distinction: A disagreement or an isolated rude comment is not automatically bullying. Serious one-off harm still deserves attention and support. Context matters.

INTERACTIVE LESSON 01

Is this bullying?

Pick a situation. Look for power, repetition and harmful behaviour. These examples teach the features — they are not diagnoses.

WHAT TO NOTICE

Repeated targeting + group power

Several students repeatedly use a chat to target one person. A pattern and social power imbalance suggest bullying. A trusted adult can help assess the situation and respond safely.

FORMS OF BULLYING
01 / VERBAL

Repeated insults, mocking or threatening words.

02 / SOCIAL

Deliberate exclusion, rumours and damaged relationships.

03 / PHYSICAL

Repeated intimidation, aggression or misuse of physical power.

04 / DIGITAL

Repeated targeting through messages, games, groups and platforms.

05 / DISCRIMINATORY

Bullying that targets identity or perceived differences.

02 / INTERVENECHOICES CAN CHANGE THE NEXT STEP.

A message spreads.
So can support.

Being a bystander does not mean being powerless. Forwarding harmful content can widen its reach. Choosing not to share, checking in privately or asking for help can be safer ways to respond.

See StopBullying.gov's bystander guidance ↗

What happens if you act?

Select a fictional bystander response. The network illustrates the possible pathway; the explanation tells you why the choice matters.

CHOICE 01 / AMPLIFICATION

Sharing adds another route.

Passing along hurtful content can expose more people to it. You can choose not to become another link in that chain.

BE AN UPSTANDER
“You don’t need to be the loudest person in the group to make a safer choice.”

Not engaging, showing support privately, or getting trusted help can all matter. Direct confrontation is not required and may not be safe.

RESEARCH NOTE / HONESTY MATTERS

Studies about in-person bystander intervention cannot automatically predict what works online. This site does not assign success percentages to the four choices; they are illustrative teaching scenarios based on safety guidance.

03 / SUPPORTYOU CAN ASK FOR HELP AT ANY STAGE.

The next step is not yours alone.

Support is not just about making content disappear. It is about safety, being heard and getting practical help. eSafety research found that only about half of young people who experienced cyberbullying told someone — making help-seeking an important part of the conversation.

FIG. 03 / A PRACTICAL PATHWAY

A safer response, step by step.

These options are not rigid rules or an emergency checklist. You can ask for help at any point.

01 / PRESERVE

Keep the information that helps explain what happened.

Where safe, save relevant screenshots, links, usernames and dates. Avoid redistributing harmful content to others. A trusted adult can help you decide what evidence is useful.

eSafety evidence guide ↗
CONNECTION IS PRACTICAL

Checking in can be a beginning.

If you are targeted, telling a trusted adult, school counsellor or support service can help you work out the next move. If you are a witness, you can offer to listen or to find support together.

Some situations need adult or professional involvement. Nobody should be pressured to confront another person directly.
